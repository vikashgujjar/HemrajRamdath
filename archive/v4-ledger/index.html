Beyond Indentureship

Indo-Trinidadian Entrepreneurs (2023) · Commentary by Dr. Bhoendradatt Tewarie

Growing up in Penal, Raj watched his grandparents, who never attended school, run a market stall and a cocoa and coffee estate for more than fifty years. That business paid for the education of most of their ten children. He wanted to know why stories like theirs were not taught in schools or business programmes.

The answer is this book: case studies of Indo-Trinidadian family businesses, from legacy firms founded during indentureship and still operating today, such as Battoo Brothers, to household names like S.M. Jaleel, Sacha Cosmetics, Charran’s and The Chancellor Hotel. A dedicated section profiles pioneering Indian women entrepreneurs, including Helen Bhagwansingh and Madame Ramdoolarie Maharaj.

“Why is this history of local entrepreneurship not better known nor taught in schools or in our universities?”

Hemraj “Raj” Ramdath is a Canadian-based business consultant, researcher and author who has spent four decades working globally in the energy and fast-moving consumer goods sectors.

He began his career with the United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America (ECLA) and went on to hold executive roles in strategy, branding, global market entry, innovation and business development. These included Barbados Country Manager with Shell, Vice President of Global Business at S.M. Jaleel & Co. Ltd., and Vice President of Strategy and Business Development at Petrotrin.

He is a charter member of the Rotary Club of Barbados South, a former member of the Rotary Club of Port of Spain, a life member of the National Council of Indian Culture and a board member of Kathak Kala Sangam Inc. He has presented papers on indentureship and entrepreneurship at Global Indian Diaspora conferences.
