Foreword by Dr. Bhoendradatt Tewarie, former Principal of The University of the West Indies

The world drinks Caribbean rum, wears Caribbean beauty, dances to Caribbean music, and watches Caribbean athletes rewrite the record books. Yet the region has rarely told how these brands were built.

The Caribbean’s 50 Most Iconic Brands tells that story through 50 cases, documenting how firms and individuals from small island economies built names that compete and win worldwide.

§ 03About the author

Hemraj Ramdath

Author of The Caribbean’s 50 Most Iconic Brands

Hemraj “Raj” Ramdath is a Canada-based management consultant, researcher and author whose career over the past four decades has been focused on building brands and taking them into new markets.

He grew up in the rural town of Penal, Trinidad, where, at the age of eight, he helped his grandparents, who had never attended school, run produce stalls in the public markets of Marabella, Penal, Fyzabad and Siparia. That family business paid for the education of most of their ten children and gave him an early lesson in Caribbean enterprise.

He began his career with the United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America (ECLA) and later held senior executive roles in multinational and regional organizations, where he led initiatives in corporate strategy, brand development, global market entry, innovation and business expansion, with brand reputation and growth at the heart of his work. His roles included Barbados Country Manager at Shell, Vice President of Global Business at S.M. Jaleel & Co. Ltd., and Vice President of Business Strategy and Business Development at the Petroleum Company of Trinidad and Tobago.

Brands he helped build

  • Shelltox
  • Chubby
  • Squeezy
  • Bico
  • Banks
  • Lasco

He led teams that introduced and grew these brands across the Caribbean and beyond. Several connect directly to brands profiled in The Caribbean’s 50 Most Iconic Brands: Chubby and Banks appear in their own right, Shell Caribbean is now Sol Petroleum, and Squeezy belongs to the ANSA McAL group, owner of Carib Beer.

Today, he is building Karibbean Flavours and oversees a Miami-based distribution centre that helps Caribbean brands enter and grow in the North American market.

Ramdath holds a BA with combined honours in Political Science and Economics, as well as an Executive MBA. He has a long record of mentorship and human capital development, including work in Cameroon, and remains closely engaged in mentoring business students and emerging entrepreneurs worldwide.

A committed Rotarian for over four decades, he is a past President of the Rotary Club of Orleans, Ottawa, and a Charter Member of the Rotary Club of Barbados South. He is a Life Member of the National Council of Indian Culture.

He is a sought-after speaker on indentureship and entrepreneurship, addressing audiences at high schools, colleges, universities and Global Indian Diaspora conferences around the world. His first book, Beyond Indentureship: Indo-Trinidadian Entrepreneurs (2023), documents 33 Indian-owned family businesses built by the descendants of indentured labourers in Trinidad. The Caribbean’s 50 Most Iconic Brands (2026) extends that work across the region, proudly profiling and celebrating fifty brands from twelve countries that have brought Caribbean excellence onto the world stage.

He lives with his family in Ottawa, Canada.
